Marketing ROI Dashboard

Whole-business return on spend, by Australian financial year

The ROI page lives under Marketing on the MAG Metrics Dashboard. Unlike the ROAS tab — which looks only at paid-ad spend and the leads it won — the ROI page takes a whole-of-business view: every dollar of revenue against every dollar of spend, pulled straight from MAG’s Xero accounts and framed by Australian financial year. It answers one question: for the money the business spent, what did it get back?

What the page shows

Everything on the page is framed by financial year (FY) and refreshes when you change the FY filter at the top. There are seven headline cards and a set of charts.

The seven cards

  • Total revenue — all income for the year.
  • Marketing spend — total business spend for the year (see the caveat below — this is whole-business, not just ads).
  • Return on spend — revenue ÷ spend, shown as a multiple (e.g. 1.32×).
  • Net ROI — the same thing as a percentage (e.g. +32%).
  • Clients won — the number of deals won in the year.
  • Cost per client (CAC) — spend ÷ clients won.
  • Avg revenue per client — revenue ÷ clients won.

The charts

  • Revenue vs spend by month — a combo chart: green spend bars and a blue revenue line, so you can see the two move against each other through the year.
  • Deals by pipeline — won / open / lost for each sales pipeline, with a “Won only” toggle to strip it back to just the wins.
  • Where the spend goes — a breakdown of spend by expense category.
  • Channel Summary — a monthly breakdown of marketing spend by channel, with a By type / By platform toggle.
  • ROI by FY table — every financial year side by side.

How to read it

Financial years

MAG’s financial year runs July to June and is labelled by the year it ends — so FY27 means July 2026 to June 2027. The page opens on the current financial year; use the filter bar at the top to switch years or see all years at once.

Owner wages are always left out

The money figures exclude the directors’ own wages and super (James & Mia). This is deliberate: it shows the operating profitability of the business before the owners pay themselves. With owner pay counted in, the business runs close to break-even (around 0.96–0.99×); with it excluded — the view this page takes — return on spend sits around 1.2–1.45×.

Toggles

  • “Won only” on the pipeline chart — hides open and lost deals.
  • By type / By platform on the Channel Summary chart — groups marketing spend either by channel type or by the specific platform.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on the page traces back to one of three sources. Nothing is typed in by hand or estimated — it is read live from the systems MAG already uses.

Figure 1 — which source feeds each card and chart.

  • Revenue comes from the Xero profit & loss (“Total Income”, excluding GST), month by month.
  • Total spend is all expenses from the same Xero P&L — operating expenses plus cost of sales, across the whole business.
  • Deals (clients won, open and lost, and the pipeline chart) come from ActiveCampaign, MAG’s CRM.
  • Channel Summary comes from Xero bank transactions on the marketing accounts, sorted into channels using a vendor map.

The page currently covers FY22 through the current year, month by month.

Key things to know

A few numbers can look surprising at first glance. These are all correct and expected:

  • This is whole-business spend, not ad spend. The “Marketing spend”, CAC and ROI cards cover all company spend — not just advertising. For paid-ad performance, use the ROAS tab instead.
  • The current year is only part-way through. Its spend (and revenue) will be lower than a full year because the year isn’t finished yet.
  • Some months show a negative spend bar. Xero books the owners’ whole-year wages in a single June entry, and year-end accounting adjustments can land in one month — so a single month can read negative. It always nets out correctly across the full year.
  • FY22 shows a very low cost per client. That year had a lot of small COVID-era virtual deals, so the client count is unusually high. The revenue and spend are solid — it’s just the per-client averages that read low.

How it’s built

The ROI page is part of the MAG Metrics Dashboard — a single web app that serves both the pages you see and the data behind them. The figures are calculated fresh each time the page loads, from a copy of MAG’s data held in a database.

Figure 2 — the end-to-end flow from source systems to the page.

The pipeline, step by step

  • Source systems — Xero (accounts) and ActiveCampaign (CRM) hold the original data.
  • Database (Supabase/Postgres) — the dashboard keeps its own copies: a monthly revenue-and-spend table from the Xero P&L, the ActiveCampaign deal list, and a marketing-spend-by-vendor table.
  • Channel sync — a small scheduled job pulls the marketing bank transactions from Xero once a month and sorts them into channels.
  • API — when the page loads, it calls two endpoints that run the calculations (revenue ÷ spend, per-FY totals, per-pipeline counts) and return the results.
  • The page — roi.html draws the cards and charts from that response, and does the owner-wage exclusion and the ratios in the browser.

Tech stack

  • Frontend: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (no build step), with Chart.js for the charts.
  • Backend: a Node.js / Express service that computes the metrics from the database on request.
  • Database: Supabase (hosted PostgreSQL).
  • Scheduled sync: a Supabase Edge Function (Deno) that refreshes the channel data monthly.
  • Data sources: Xero (connected via a free Xero web-app login) and ActiveCampaign.
  • Hosting: Google Cloud Run.

How fresh the data is

  • The revenue and spend figures are a loaded copy of the Xero P&L; an automatic refresh is still to be set up, so they are updated on a periodic manual reload for now.
  • The Channel Summary refreshes monthly on a schedule.
  • The deal counts follow the dashboard’s daily ActiveCampaign sync.
