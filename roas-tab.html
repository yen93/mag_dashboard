The ROAS Tab

A plain-English guide to the dashboard's paid-search Return on Ad Spend page

The ROAS tab answers one question: for every dollar MAG spends on paid search, how much won business comes back? It reports Return on Ad Spend by Australian financial year, computed live from the company database — so the figures refresh themselves every time the page is opened. This guide covers what’s on the page, how to read it, and how it’s put together behind the scenes.

What the ROAS tab is for

ROAS stands for Return on Ad Spend — how much won revenue each dollar of advertising brings in. The tab brings MAG’s paid-search advertising and the deals it produced side by side, so you can see whether the spend is paying off.

  • Everything is grouped by Australian financial year (July–June). Years are named by the year they end — so FY25 means July 2024 to June 2025.
  • The headline measure is ROAS: won revenue divided by what was spent. The target is 2.00× — two dollars of won business for every dollar spent.
  • The page is live: it reads the database when it loads, so you’re always looking at current figures (cached for only a few minutes at a time).

Think of it as a fast, always-on cross-check of how paid search is performing — not the formal, GST-adjusted audited ROAS report, which uses slightly different numbers.

Getting around the page

From top to bottom, here’s what you’ll find and how to use it.

The controls (top bar)

  • Financial year — pick a single FY, or “All years” for the combined picture. The whole page re-filters to your choice.
  • Cost basis — switch between All-in (Google + Bing + Simple Digital) and Google Ads only. This changes the ROAS, cost and ROI figures.
  • A one-line takeaway sums up the selected year in plain words (for example, “$2.40 back for every $1”).

The KPI cards

  • ROAS, with a green pill if it clears the 2.00× target or a red one if it misses.
  • Paid-search cost for the year, broken down by channel.
  • Won revenue from ads, and the number of won deals.
  • Won, Lost and Open pipeline tiles — click any of these to jump straight to the matching deals in the table below.

The charts

  • ROAS by FY — a bar per year against the dashed 2.00× target line; bars that clear the target are cyan, those that miss are red.
  • What we spent vs what we won — the cost (stacked by channel) next to the won revenue for each year, so the gap is easy to see.
  • Clicking a bar selects that financial year for the whole page.

The tables

  • ROAS by FY — the full breakdown, one row per year plus an “All years” total. Click a row to filter the page to that year.
  • Deals — the individual deals behind the numbers. Filter by Won / Lost / Open / All, or search by organisation, deal or comment.

The assistant

A read-only chat helper sits in the bottom-right corner. Ask it questions about the figures on the tab (“Which year had the best all-in ROAS?”). It can only read this tab’s data — it can’t change anything.

What each number means

A quick glossary for the figures in the cards and tables.

  • ROAS (Google) = won revenue ÷ Google Ads spend.
  • ROAS (all-in) = won revenue ÷ (Google + Bing + Simple Digital) spend.
  • Won / Lost / Open = the count of counted deals in each state for the year.
  • Win rate = won ÷ (won + lost + open).
  • Won revenue = the contracted value of the won deals (credited to the financial year the enquiry first came in).
  • Open pipeline = the value of open deals that haven’t closed yet (not counted as won).
  • ROI $ = won revenue − cost, on the selected basis. ROI % is the same as a percentage of cost.
  • Per-Lead Value = won revenue ÷ (won + lost + open).

Only financial years that actually had ad spend are shown. Years with less than a full 12 months of spend (the earliest year, and the current one in progress) are flagged as partial, so a short year isn’t mistaken for a weak one.

Where the figures come from

The tab stitches two eras together so that every financial year is on one chart, even though the older and newer years are tracked in different places.

Figure 1 — how each financial year is sourced.

  • FY22–FY24 come from Niki’s Google Ads lead log — the deal counts and won revenue, plus the Google spend (added up from the per-lead click cost). Simple Digital’s fee is picked up from Xero billing history; Bing reads $0 because MAG wasn’t running Bing Ads yet.
  • FY25 onward take their deal counts from the ROAS audit (deals tagged “Google Ads = Yes”), plus any JC manual tags and GA4 paid-click leads. The spend comes from live monthly figures: Google from the Google Ads account, Bing and Simple Digital billed through Xero.

All of this flows through one canonical list of counted deals in the database, so the ROAS tab, the deals table and the GA4 & Ads Leads page’s “ROAS-counted only” view all reconcile to the same figures.

The two cost bases

The cost-basis toggle changes which costs count against the same won revenue.

Figure 2 — the two ways cost is defined.

  • Google Ads only compares won revenue against just the Google Ads spend — useful for judging the Google campaigns on their own.
  • All-in compares it against the full paid-search cost: Google plus Bing plus the Simple Digital management fee — the truer picture of what paid search really costs.

Either way, the target is 2.00×. A green pill means the year cleared it; a red pill means it fell short.

How it’s built

The ROAS tab is one page of MAG’s internal metrics dashboard. Nothing is pre-baked: when you open it, the page asks the server for the numbers, and the server works them out from the database on the spot.

Figure 3 — the end-to-end data flow, from raw sources to the page.

The pipeline, end to end

  • Deal sources — Niki’s Google Ads log, the Clover ROAS audit, the GA4 & Ads leads table, and the ActiveCampaign deals mirror — feed a single canonical “counted deals” database view. It keeps one row per deal, removes duplicates, and resolves each deal’s financial year and value.
  • Ad-spend tables hold the monthly Google, Bing and Simple Digital costs for the newer years.
  • The Express API groups the counted-deals view by financial year, adds the spend, and works out ROAS, win rate and the rest — served at /api/operations/roas and /api/operations/roas/deals.
  • The ROAS tab page takes that and draws the charts, KPI cards and tables.

Keeping the spend figures fresh

  • Google Ads monthly cost is pulled from the Google Ads account by a scheduled database job once a week.
  • Bing and Simple Digital costs are read from reconciled Xero bank transactions by another scheduled job each week.

The tech stack

  • Plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript — no framework and no build step, so the page stays hand-editable.
  • Chart.js for the charts.
  • Node.js + Express for the API.
  • Supabase (hosted PostgreSQL) for the data, with a canonical database view as the single source of truth.
  • Supabase Edge Functions (Deno / TypeScript), on a weekly schedule, to refresh the ad-spend figures.
  • The Google Ads API (Google spend) and the Xero API (Bing + Simple Digital spend).
  • OpenAI powers the read-only chat assistant.
  • Google Cloud Run hosts the whole service.

Good to know

  • These figures are a live cross-check, not the formal audited ROAS report. The audited report uses GST-adjusted values, so its totals will differ slightly.
  • Revenue is credited to the financial year the enquiry came in, not the year the deal closed — that’s deliberate, so the return lines up with the spend that generated it.
  • Deal values are the contracted amounts from the CRM, not billed revenue.
  • The deals table shows the counted deals — the exact set the ROAS figures are built from.
