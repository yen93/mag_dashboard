The ROAS Tab

Return on paid-search ad spend, by financial year

The ROAS tab answers one question for MAG's paid search: for every dollar we put into Google Ads, how many dollars of won business came back? It lives under Marketing in the MAG Metrics dashboard, is computed live from our own data every time you open it, and breaks the answer down by Australian financial year — so you can see whether paid search is paying for itself, and how that is trending.

What the ROAS tab shows

When you open the tab it loads the latest figures and leads with a one-line takeaway, e.g. “FY26: $2.40 back for every $1 of paid search, all-in — that clears the $2 target.” Below that are four kinds of view:

  • Headline KPI tiles — ROAS for the selected year, the paid-search cost, the won revenue credited to ads, and the Won / Lost / Open deal counts.
  • ROAS by FY chart — a bar per financial year against the dashed 2.00× target line; bars that clear the target are blue, bars that miss are red.
  • What we spent vs what we won chart — cost stacked by channel (Google, Bing, Simple Digital) sitting beside the won revenue for each year.
  • Two tables — a wide ROAS by FY summary table, and a deals table listing the individual deals behind the numbers.

How to use it

Pick a financial year

Use the Financial year buttons at the top (All years, or a single FY). You can also click any bar in a chart or any row in the ROAS-by-FY table to jump straight to that year — the whole page re-filters to it.

Switch the cost basis

The Cost basis toggle changes what counts as “spend”:

  • All-in — Google Ads + Bing + Simple Digital fees (the true cost of paid search).
  • Google Ads only — just the Google Ads cost.

Every ROAS, ROI and cost figure on the page follows the basis you pick.

Drill into the deals

Click the Won, Lost or Open pipeline KPI tile to scroll to the deals table, already filtered to that status. In the deals table you can filter by status (Won / Lost / Open / All) and by scope — Counted as Google Ads, Needs review, or Tagged, not counted — and search by organisation, deal or comment.

The 2.00× target and the key numbers

The target is 2.00× — two dollars of won business for every dollar spent. The main figures are defined as:

  • ROAS = won revenue ÷ paid-search cost (on the selected basis).
  • Win rate = won deals ÷ (won + lost + open).
  • Per-Lead Value = won revenue ÷ (won + lost + open).
  • ROI ($) = won revenue − cost; ROI % expresses that against the cost.

Revenue is always credited to the financial year the enquiry came in, not the year it eventually closed — so a year’s ROAS reflects the leads that year’s spend generated.

Where the numbers come from

The tab is a live cross-check, rebuilt from the database on every visit. The sources it reads depend on the financial year, because MAG tracked paid search differently before and after FY25.

Figure 1 — each ROAS figure is assembled live by roas.js from the source tables, then served to the page.

FY22–FY24 — Niki’s lead log

  • Spend = the summed per-lead click cost from Niki’s Google Ads lead log.
  • Won / Lost / Open and won revenue come from the deals recorded against those leads.
  • FY22 is a partial year — the data only starts in May 2022.

FY25+ — live tables and the audited deal set

  • Cost = live monthly Google Ads cost, plus Bing and Simple Digital fees. Bing and Simple Digital currently read $0 until their syncs are connected, so until then the all-in basis equals Google-only.
  • Won / Lost / Open and revenue come from the counted set — see Figure 2.

Deals titled “Uniting…” are treated as referrals and left out of the counts.

How it's built

The ROAS tab is one page of the MAG Metrics dashboard — a hand-editable web app (plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript, no build step) served by a small API, both running as a single service on Google Cloud Run and backed by MAG’s Supabase database.

The pipeline, end to end

  • Source tables (Supabase) — Niki’s lead log for the older years, and the live Google/Bing/Simple-Digital cost tables plus the audited and GA4-matched deal tables for FY25+.
  • roas.js (the calculator) — a server module that runs one SQL query to group every source by financial year, works out both cost bases, and shapes the KPIs, chart series and table rows.
  • The API — /api/operations/roas serves the yearly summary and /api/operations/roas/deals serves the individual deals, each cached briefly.
  • roas.html (the page) — draws the tiles, charts and tables in the browser and handles the year / cost-basis / deal filters.
  • A weekly refresh — a scheduled job keeps the live Google Ads cost up to date in the background.

The tech stack

  • Supabase (PostgreSQL) — the database the figures are queried from.
  • Node.js / Express — the API service.
  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript — the page itself, with no framework or bundler.
  • Chart.js — the ROAS and spend-vs-won charts.
  • Supabase Edge Functions & scheduled jobs — the background refresh of ad-spend data.
  • Google Cloud Run — hosts the whole service.

Good to know

  • This is a live cross-check, not the formal audited ROAS report. It is designed to stay close to the audit, but the exact dollar figures can differ.
  • Bing and Simple Digital read $0 until their data syncs are switched on; while that is the case the all-in cost basis matches Google-only.
  • Partial years are flagged (FY22, and the current year until it has a full 12 months of spend), so a short year isn’t mistaken for a weak one.
  • Only financial years that actually have ad spend appear.
